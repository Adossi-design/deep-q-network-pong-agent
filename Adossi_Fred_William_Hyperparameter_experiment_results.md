Every run trained for 500,000 steps with the same seed, and the average reward quoted for each one comes from its last 100 episodes in the log file. Those rewards were recorded while the agent was still choosing some random moves, so they are a little lower than what the model would score when it plays greedily.

| Experiment | Learning rate | Gamma | Batch size | Epsilon start | Epsilon end | Epsilon decay | Observed behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0001 | 0.99 | 32 | 1.0 | 0.01 | 100000 | This is the baseline and it ended with an average reward of -17.71. The reward sat near -20.5 for the first 300,000 steps and then began to climb slowly, which tells me the agent had only just started to return the ball and was still losing most points. |
| 2 | 0.0005 | 0.99 | 32 | 1.0 | 0.01 | 100000 | The average reward was -20.95 and it never moved away from the bottom, even drifting down a little as the run went on. A learning rate five times bigger most likely made every update too large, so the network kept overshooting and never settled. |
| 3 | 0.00001 | 0.99 | 32 | 1.0 | 0.01 | 100000 | It finished at -20.51 and stayed almost flat from start to end. With a learning rate ten times smaller the weights changed so slowly that 500,000 steps were simply not enough to learn anything useful. |
| 4 | 0.0001 | 0.95 | 32 | 1.0 | 0.01 | 100000 | The average reward was -16.18 and it kept rising, finishing a little better than the baseline. A lower gamma makes the agent care less about rewards far in the future, and that probably made the targets steadier early in training. |
| 5 | 0.0001 | 0.90 | 32 | 1.0 | 0.01 | 100000 | It ended at -14.86, still rising, and better than both of the higher gamma runs. In Pong the reward comes only a few moves after the paddle hits the ball, so looking a short way ahead seems to be enough. |
| 6 | 0.0001 | 0.99 | 64 | 1.0 | 0.01 | 100000 | The average reward was -13.40 and it rose steadily, beating the baseline by about four points. A bigger batch averages over more experiences at each update, so the gradient is less noisy and the network learns more smoothly. |
| 7 | 0.0001 | 0.99 | 128 | 1.0 | 0.01 | 100000 | This was the best run with -7.28. Its reward rose faster than any other and it jumped around the most, with one episode reaching +15, so the agent was already winning some games. The very large batch gave the cleanest gradients of all my runs. |
| 8 | 0.0001 | 0.99 | 32 | 0.5 | 0.01 | 100000 | It finished at -17.77, almost exactly the same as the baseline, and the reward rose just as slowly. Starting epsilon at 0.5 instead of 1.0 hardly mattered because epsilon falls quickly in the first part of the run anyway. |
| 9 | 0.0001 | 0.99 | 32 | 1.0 | 0.1 | 100000 | The average reward was -16.93 and it kept rising, about one point better than the baseline, which is small enough that a different seed could erase it. Because epsilon stopped at 0.1, one move in ten stayed random, and that held the score down a little. |
| 10 | 0.0001 | 0.99 | 32 | 1.0 | 0.01 | 250000 | It ended at -20.14 and stayed nearly flat. With such a slow decay the agent kept acting mostly at random until step 250,000, so it had only half the run left to use what it had learned. |

The best experiment was number 7, where the bigger batch took the average reward to -7.28 while the baseline stayed at -17.71. The worst was number 2, which never left about -21 because the learning rate was too high, and numbers 3 and 10 also stayed close to the bottom for the reasons given above. Across all ten runs a larger batch helped the most, a lower gamma helped a little, and the starting and ending values of epsilon changed very little. Since each run used only one seed, gaps of a point or two, like those between numbers 1, 8 and 9, could easily be noise, and numbers 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7 were still improving when they stopped, so none of them had finished learning. For the final model I would pick the settings of experiment 7, because it learned much faster than the rest and was already beating the computer in some games. Pairing it with a gamma of 0.90 looks like a sensible next test, although I have not run that combination.
